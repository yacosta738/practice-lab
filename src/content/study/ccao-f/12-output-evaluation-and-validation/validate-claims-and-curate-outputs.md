> Judge an answer against the task and its evidence. Fluency and a citation do not establish that a
> claim is accurate, complete, or appropriate to use.

**Blueprint area:** D2 - Output Evaluation and Validation (official domain weight 21%).

## Review the result against the request

Output review has several parts. Check whether the response:

- answers the requested question and follows the requested format;
- includes all required points, exceptions, and relevant context;
- states facts that are supported by the available evidence;
- signals uncertainty instead of filling gaps with invented detail;
- avoids unfair assumptions or unsupported generalizations; and
- is suitable for the audience and the decision that will use it.

A citation is a lead to evidence, not proof by itself. Open or inspect the cited material and check
that it supports the exact claim. A source may be real but irrelevant, incomplete, outdated, or
misread.

## Scenario

A manager asks for a summary of a travel policy, including reimbursement limits and exceptions. The
draft includes references to policy sections, but one reference discusses a different expense type
and the summary omits an exception for remote employees. The reviewer checks each material statement
against the policy, corrects the mismatched claim, adds the missing exception, and asks the policy
owner to review the final version before distribution.

## Decision guidance

1. Compare the output with the original task to find missing requirements or format errors.
2. Identify claims that could affect a decision, then trace each one to a reliable, relevant source.
3. Check ambiguous or unsupported points against another suitable source or mark them as unknown.
4. Consider whether wording, selection, or recommendations could disadvantage a group or hide an
   important alternative.
5. Use human review when the impact of an error is consequential, the evidence is uncertain, or the
   work requires domain judgment.
6. Edit for clarity and usefulness only after preserving the evidence and required caveats.

The depth of review should match the consequences of using the result. A low-risk draft may need a
focused check. A high-impact decision requires stronger evidence and a responsible human decision
maker.

## Misconceptions

- A citation does not show that the source supports the claim. Check the source and the claim
  together.
- A complete-looking response may still omit a requested exception or include an unsupported
  detail.
- One correct example does not prove that a response pattern is reliable across other cases.
- Human review is not a rubber stamp. The reviewer needs the authority, context, and time to assess
  the work.
- A raw percentage on a practice activity is not the official exam's scaled score. The official
  CCAO-F exam reports a scaled score; the custom Practice Lab mock is separate and its raw result
  does not predict that score.

## Self-check

- Can you point to source material that supports each important factual claim, not just a citation
  label?
- What requested detail is missing, uncertain, or important enough to require human review?
